## (a) What Project Rakshak is

- CFI's **flagship youth-led road safety & infrastructure initiative**, launched **July 2025** by Crashfree India (S1). Conceptualized/guided by **Dr. Geetam Tiwari** (Emeritus Professor, TRIP Centre, IIT Delhi) and **Dr. Richa Ahuja** (Asst. Prof., IIT Kharagpur; Programme Advisor).
- Core model: **student teams** (mostly engineering students at IITs/NITs/SPAs etc.) identify high crash-risk locations ("blackspots" and emerging micro-risk sites), conduct field road-safety audits + stakeholder surveys, compile crash data (FIRs, RTI, police records, media), design interventions aligned with **IRC and MoRTH** standards, and formally present authority-ready reports to implementing agencies to secure **written government approvals** — then track through to **implementation**.
- Mentor Manas Choudhary's foreword: the program's roots go back "a decade" as a campus-level road-safety effort that matured into Rakshak.
- **Two verticals (as of Cohort 2, S2):**
  1. **Student Vertical** — individual volunteers/college teams conduct mentored road audits under faculty + CFI guidance. Youth-anchored, college-based.
  2. **Expert Vertical** (Cohort 2 centrepiece) — certified Road Safety Experts do end-to-end audit → analysis → implementation work under a **formal, funded MoU** with CFI. Flow: Expert Partner → CFI Technical Advisory Panel → DRSCs/Road Authorities → Government Approval.
- Institutional web (S2 diagram): Engineering Colleges — Faculty Mentors — Student Teams — District Road Safety Committees (DRSCs) — Road Authorities / State Lead Agencies.
- Ambition: extend into **self-sustaining university chapters**; "institutionalising Rakshak as India's national youth-led road safety implementation model" (S3).
- Geography (Cohort 1): **18 cities** across India — map lists Ropar, Dehradun, Roorkee, Delhi, Lucknow, Varanasi, Durgapur, Surat, Indore, Kolkata, Mumbai, Nanded ("Nandad" in text), Vijayawada, Guntur, Kozhikode ("Koshikode"), Chennai, Itanagar, Guwahati. Spans urban, peri-urban and highway contexts, north to northeast to south.
- **Cohort-1 phases (S1):**
  - Phase 1 (Jul–Aug 2025): Research & Problem Identification — site shortlisting, crash data via FIR/RTI/police/media, weekly 1:1 mentor sessions.
  - Phase 2 (Aug–Sept 2025): Stakeholder Engagement & Road Safety Audits — 30+ surveys per team; IRC/MoRTH-aligned audit checklists; expert session by Dr. Geetam Tiwari (Safe System, speed–injury relationships, VRU prioritisation).
  - Phase 3 (Oct–Nov 2025): Case Study Analysis & Solution Development — expert session by Dr. S. Velmurugan (5Es framework, traffic composition, pedestrian-first design, IRC-131 blackspot analysis).
  - Phase 4 (Nov 2025–present): Final Reporting & Authority Engagement — authority-format reports/presentations; external domain-expert evaluation "ahead of the finale."
- **Cohort-2 / Expert phases (S2):** 1. Research & Development → Scoping Report; 2. Stakeholder Engagement & Audits → Audit Report + consultation log; 3. Analysis & Solution Development → Analysis & Solutions Report; 4. Reporting & Advocacy → Final Report + Government Approval Letter + Implementation Strategy. Progress tracked in real time on **CFI's dedicated Rakshak portal** with an assigned CFI POC.
- **Expert funding structure (S2):** two-stage disbursement — **40%** on Phase 2 completion (approved Audit Report), **60%** on Phase 4 completion (Final Report + Implementation Strategy + formal Government Approval/Acknowledgement Letter). Eligible cost heads: personnel, field data collection, travel, stakeholder engagement, minor implementation support. Per-phase actual-cost budget reporting required.
- **Expert eligibility (S2):** sector experience; qualified personnel (engineers, urban planners, researchers, field staff); government relationships (PWD / District Administration / Traffic Police pathways); methodology fit with CFI audit methodology.
- **Expert application process (S2):** Expression of Interest → Preliminary Review → Scoping Discussion → Due Diligence → MoU & portal onboarding → Project Kick-off.

## (c) How it works, step by step (student model)
1. Team forms at a college; identifies candidate high-risk locations via on-ground research + crash data (FIRs, RTIs, police records, media).
2. Site mapping, photos, preliminary risk observations; weekly 1:1 mentor reviews; end-of-phase checkpoint.
3. 30+ stakeholder surveys per team (pedestrians, commuters, residents, shopkeepers, police, authorities); formal audits with IRC/MoRTH-aligned checklists; crash-data triangulation; expert masterclasses (Tiwari, Velmurugan).
4. Each team prioritises **one** site (120+ → 31); composite 0–100 Risk Index scoring.
5. Root-cause analysis across engineering/enforcement/education/emergency-care; case-study benchmarking; prioritised, costed, standards-aligned interventions; cross-mentor reviews; some teams apply data analytics and AI/ML tools.
6. Authority-ready final reports & presentations to municipal bodies, PWD, NHAI, Traffic Police, district administrations; external expert evaluation before the finale.
7. Secure written approval/in-principle support letter from authority.
8. Implementation begins (parking, drainage, markings, signage, footpaths, barricading, lighting…); tracked via **public dashboard** crashfreeindia.org/rakshak/dashboard.
9. Accountability loop (S2): Audit Submission → Authority Review → Approval → Implementation → Public Tracking; before–after evidence builds a library of replicable case studies. "Most audit programmes stop at report submission. Rakshak tracks every recommendation through to implementation."
10. Post-approval government pipeline (S3): DPR (detailed project report with cost estimates/tech specs) → feasibility checks → government budget allocation tied to approved interventions → tendering & execution.
- QA mechanisms: continuous 1:1 mentoring, phase-wise review checkpoints, advisor-led mentor alignment sessions each phase, cross-mentor reviews, final external expert evaluation.
