## 0. The National Road Safety Board (NRSB) itself

**Statutory basis (verified):**
- Section 215B, Motor Vehicles Act 1988, inserted by the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019: provides for a National Road Safety Board to advise the Central/State Governments on all aspects of road safety and traffic management, including standards of design, construction, operation and maintenance of motor vehicles and safety equipment.
- National Road Safety Board Rules notified 3 September 2021 (PIB release PRID 1845883). The Rules empower the Board to constitute "as many Technical Working Groups as it may consider necessary for the efficient discharge of its functions."

**Constitution (verified):**
- Board formally constituted 1 July 2026, reported as being ahead of a Supreme Court deadline (road-safety PIL directions; multiple outlets: Indian Masterminds, Swarajya, Indian Mandarins, indianbureaucracy.com).
- 16-member panel: Chairman + ex-officio members + 7 independent members.

**Chairman (verified):** Dr Nitin R. Gokarn, IAS (Retd., 1990 batch, UP cadre), former Additional Chief Secretary, Government of Uttar Pradesh. Reported to have earlier served in MoRTH handling transport and road safety matters. Active on LinkedIn about NRSB (posts under "dr-nitin-r-gokarn-51711920").

**Ex-officio members (verified via Indian Mandarins summary):**
- ADG (East), MoRTH; ADG (North/Quality Control), MoRTH
- Member (Admin.) and Member (Road Safety), NHAI
- Director (Technical–I), NHIDCL
- Additional Secretary (T)/Joint Secretary (T), MoRTH — Ex-officio Member Secretary

**Independent members (verified via Indian Masterminds/Indian Mandarins):**
1. Satyendra Garg — retired IPS, enforcement/traffic management expert
2. Arushi Baluja Sahi — IRTE (Head, Driver Training, Public Health, CSR & Intl Relations)

6. K. C. Sharma — Advisor (Motor Vehicle Legislation), MoRTH
7. Dr Gautham Melur Sukumar — Professor of Epidemiology, Head, WHO Collaborating Centre for Injury Prevention and Safety Promotion, NIMHANS Bengaluru

**Six Working Groups (verified names, surfaced via Dr Gokarn's LinkedIn post, ~late July 2026, activity 7485596689517502464):**
1. Safe Road Users, Licensing and Enforcement
2. Safe Roads, Speed Management and Vulnerable Road Users (= WG-2)
3. Safe Vehicles, Commercial Transport and Future Mobility
4. Data, Crash Investigation, Research and Innovation
5. Post-Crash Care, Policy Standards and State Implementation (= WG-5)
6. Awareness and Public Outreach

**Expected WG outputs (verified language from the LinkedIn post as indexed by search):** WGs "bring together experts from government, academia, industry and civil society to develop evidence-based recommendations, technical standards and national strategies" in their areas. This matches the NRSB Rules' "Technical Working Groups" mechanism. [NOTE] Full post text could not be fetched (LinkedIn 403 via proxy); wording taken from search-engine index of the post. ## 3. Dr Gautham Melur Sukumar — Co-chair, WG-5

**Identity confidence: HIGH.**
- **Verified:** Professor of Epidemiology (earlier styled Additional Professor), and Head, WHO Collaborating Centre for Injury Prevention and Safety Promotion, NIMHANS Bengaluru. Named as one of the seven independent members of the NRSB (July 2026 constitution).
- Verified work: PI of NIMHANS–UNICEF child road safety partnership (2024–25); national report on child/adolescent road injuries (≈45 child deaths/day; ~16,443 child road fatalities in 2022, estimated ~20% under-reporting; ~5 million child non-fatal injuries needing hospitalisation annually); School Safety Assessment Tool piloted in Bengaluru and Kolar; publications on helmet-effectiveness data interpretation and need for improved injury data systems; NIMHANS "Advancing Road Safety in India — Implementation is the Key" tradition (WHO CC monographs).

**Deck implications:**
- Data-first: present surveillance-quality numbers, acknowledge under-reporting, cite MoRTH "Road Accidents in India" plus hospital-based data limitations.
- Public-health framing of post-crash care: trauma registries, injury surveillance, measurable outcomes (mortality/disability), not just infrastructure counts.
- Reference WHO frameworks (Decade of Action 2021–30, safe-system) and child/VRU injury burden; he has published on data misinterpretation, so be rigorous with denominators and claims.
