# M2 — Young Road-Safety Builders: expanded roster + a concrete engagement model

Companion to `M-youth-innovators-platform.md` (the pressure-test). That brief's verdict stands and
shapes everything here: **engage them as a TRACK under CFI, not a new org; CFI's real offer is a
route from prototype → adoption, not a stage or a prize; intergenerational, not kids-only; and build
the safeguarding/fiscal-sponsor scaffolding before funding any minor.** This doc turns that into
(1) a growing roster of real young builders and (2) a concrete way to engage them.

**Honesty caveats (standing):** no invented names/sources; every person carries a confidence tag +
source; ages are as-reported and may have advanced. Program numbers below (cohort sizes, grant
amounts, timelines) are *proposed design parameters*, not facts — tune to budget.

---

## PART A — THE NARRATIVE (three audiences, one story)

The through-line: **India has a scattered generation of young people already building for safer
roads — but their work dies at the prototype. CFI is the bridge from their idea to a road that
actually changes.** That single sentence works because CFI uniquely owns the adoption end (DRSCs,
traffic police MoUs, CRRI/expert consortium, OEM/Cars24 line, a public accountability dashboard).

**A1. Internal / donor framing (why CFI, why now)**
> "Every year, Indian teenagers invent seatbelt locks, helmet interlocks, crash-detection apps and
> blackspot maps — and every year those ideas vanish at the science-fair table. CFI already turns
> *student road-audits* into government-approved engineering works. We can do the same for *student
> inventors*: give the best young road-safety builders an expert mentor, a small milestone grant,
> and — the part no one else can offer — a real door into the agencies and companies that deploy.
> Not a talent show. A pipeline from prototype to public good, and into CFI's own next generation."

*Fundable one-liner:* **"Rakshak Young Builders — India's bridge from young road-safety inventors to
adopted, on-road solutions."**

**A2. Student-facing invitation (the promise, in their language)**
> "You built something to make roads safer. Most people will clap and move on. We won't. Crashfree
> India works inside the system that actually fixes roads — traffic police, district road-safety
> committees, national agencies, carmakers. Join Rakshak Young Builders and we'll pair you with an
> expert who's done it, help fund your next real step, put your work on a national stage (yes, with
> MS Dhoni in our corner), and — if it's ready — open the doors to get it *tried on a real road or in
> a real vehicle.* You keep building your thing. We help it leave the shelf."

*What they get that a science fair can't give:* a mentor who's shipped, a small grant tied to real
milestones, introductions to people who deploy, a possible pilot in a CFI district, a national
platform, and a peer network of other young builders.

**A3. Public / press framing (feeds "Safety is the New Swag")**
> The "young changemakers of Indian road safety," backed by CFI + Dhoni. The *Avengers* energy lives
> **here — in the storytelling** — while the program itself runs, soberly, as a CFI track. (Per the
> pressure-test: keep the superhero narrative as a campaign layer; don't make it a separate org or
> force everyone to "build one thing together" — that joint-build almost never works.)

**Naming (pick one; recommend #1):**
1. **Rakshak Young Builders** *(recommended)* — rides existing "Be a Rakshak" equity; signals building + protection; no new brand to fund.
2. **Young Rakshaks** — punchier, more "team/Avengers," slightly more juvenile.
3. **CFI Road Safety Innovators' Circle** — most neutral/institutional; least emotional pull.

## PART B — THE ENGAGEMENT MODEL (concrete, staged)

Designed so you can engage builders **immediately and cheaply** (Stages 0–1) and only take on the
heavy machinery (money, minors, pilots) for a selective few (Stage 2). This sequencing is the whole
trick: it de-risks safeguarding and budget while still creating momentum now.

### Stage 0 — Discovery (build a living radar)
- **This roster (Part C) is the seed.** Keep it as a living list; add continuously.
- **Feeders instead of a cold open call:** INSPIRE-MANAK/NIF & IGNITE, Atal Tinkering Labs, IRSC
  campus chapters (CFI-adjacent — founders overlap), Global Youth Coalition (via Deepanshu), Smart
  India Hackathon road-safety statements, university incubators, Shark Tank-adjacent youth. Ask each
  to *refer* their road-safety kids to you.
- One annual **"Build for Safer Roads" call** + rolling self/mentor/partner nominations.

### Stage 1 — Engage & onboard (relationship before money; safeguarding-light)
The fastest, lowest-risk value. Do this **now** with the confirmed names.
- **Start with CFI's own NextMile 2025 finalists** (Safer Miles Initiative et al.) — already road-safety-committed and CFI-warm; pull names from internal records. Warmest possible first cohort.
- **Warm, specific outreach** ("we saw *Roadyz* / *Vision Guard* — here's a concrete offer"). Template in Part E.
- **Low first ask:** invite to the Rakshak/NextMile forum, an expert masterclass, and a **Young
  Builders community** (WhatsApp/Slack). No grant, no contract — so under-18s are easy here.
- **Recognition:** feature them on CFI channels + "Safety is the New Swag"; a "Young Builder" badge.
  Cheap, motivating; needs only guardian media-consent for minors.
- **Mentor intro:** one call with a consortium expert. Often the single most valued thing.

### Stage 2 — Accelerate (selective, resourced — the real bet)
Cohort of ~**8–12/yr**, selected on **one criterion above all: a credible ADOPTION PATHWAY** — who
would deploy this, and what's the next real step. (Not "coolest gadget." Fund routes to a road, not
prototypes.)
CFI provides, per builder:
1. **An expert mentor** from the Rakshak consortium (intergenerational — CFI's proven pattern).
2. **A small, milestone-based micro-grant** *(design param: ₹50k–₹2L; disbursed e.g. 40/60 on
   milestones, mirroring Rakshak expert MoUs)* — **fiscal-sponsored / guardian-routed for minors.**
3. **Doors** — targeted intros to DRSCs, traffic police (Gurugram/Jaipur MoUs), CRRI, standards
   bodies, or an OEM/Cars24 line, matched to the project.
4. **A pilot slot** in a CFI anchor district (Gurugram / SW Delhi / Jaipur) where the idea fits real
   infrastructure or enforcement work.
5. **Platform** — the national forum stage + Dhoni narrative + CFI distribution.
- **Time-boxed** (~4–6 months) → **demo day at the Rakshak/NextMile forum.**

### Stage 3 — Sustain (the durable asset the evidence says actually lasts)
- **Alumni network, actively invested in** (the long-term value, per §5 of the pressure-test — not a
  fixed cohort that ages out).
- **Pipeline into CFI:** Rakshak teams, the Safer Mobility Impact Fellowship, hiring, IRSC chapters.
- **Multi-year support** for the strongest 2–3 (multi-touch beats one-shot prizes — "Dump the Prizes").

### Guardrails (baked in, non-negotiable)
- **Safeguarding policy + guardian/fiscal-sponsor structure BEFORE any minor is funded or publicised**
  (minors can't sign contracts or hold grants). Consider **16+ for funded/pilot work**, with a
  **recognition-only "Juniors" tier** for younger kids.
- **No forced joint-build** — each builds their own; "together" = shared community, mentors,
  occasional joint campaigns, or deliberate 2–5-person module teams only.
- **Real decision power + real resources** (anti-tokenism / anti-youthwashing).
- **Free to participate; tools/kit provided** (equity — don't price out the builders you want).

### North star & metrics (avoid the vanity trap)
- **Primary:** # of youth-built interventions **piloted or adopted** by a public/OEM partner.
- **Secondary:** pipeline conversions into Rakshak/SMIF/hiring; mentor-hours delivered; # builders
  reaching a defined "next real step." **Not** follower counts.

### C5 — Honest read on the pool + a discovery implication
- **The "seatbelt kid" does not exist in accessible sources** — two independent searches found **no credible named *Indian* seatbelt inventor.** The only real named teen seatbelt inventor is Emirati (Khalid Al Shammari). Treat that slot as unfilled unless you have a private lead.
- **The verifiable named pool is genuinely thin** beyond ~15–20 people — and skewed to *helmet interlocks, drowsiness alarms, and hot-car alerts* (many kids reinvent the same 2–3 devices). India's youth-innovation portals (INSPIRE-MANAK, NIF/IGNITE, ATL, APCOST, NCERT NCSE) index *thousands* of projects but rarely expose named road-safety ones to web search.
- **Implication for Stage 0 (Discovery):** the highest-yield sourcing is **partnering with the award bodies directly** (NIF/IGNITE, INSPIRE-MANAK state cells, APCOST, Atal Innovation Mission, NCERT) to get named road-safety project lists — not web scraping. This *reinforces* the "feeders, not a cold call" design in Part B. It also means: **plan for a curated cohort of ~15–25 known builders, not a mass platform** — the pool size fits a *track*, not an *org* (exactly the pressure-test's conclusion).

## PART D — FIRST 90 DAYS (concrete)
- **Weeks 1–2:** finalise the roster; **recover NextMile 2025 finalist names from internal records**;
  pick 5–8 warm anchors (NextMile teams + Surya, Rohit Raj, Vanisha, Sri Chandana). Draft the
  safeguarding + media-consent one-pager and the fiscal-sponsor route for minors.
- **Weeks 3–4:** personal outreach (Part E); open the **Young Builders community**; first mentor intros.
- **Month 2:** publish micro-grant criteria (adoption-pathway required); confirm 3–4 consortium mentors;
  secure 1–2 concrete "doors" (a DRSC / traffic-police / OEM contact willing to review a youth project).
- **Month 3:** run a small **pilot call** for Stage-2; feature 2–3 anchors publicly as proof (Surya
  et al.); slot a Young Builders demo into the next Rakshak/NextMile forum.

**Budget sketch (design param, tune to appetite):** a lean first year — recognition + community +
mentorship + ~6–10 milestone micro-grants + one demo day — is a **small ring-fenced line**, not a
new bet. Deliberately capped so it stays a *track*, not an org.
