## Email 1 — Working Group 2

**Subject:** Expression of Interest of Crashfree India — Working Group 2: Safe Roads, Speeds & Vulnerable Road Users

Respected Chairperson and Members of Working Group 2,

Crashfree India would like to submit its Expression of Interest for induction into Working Group 2 of the National Road Safety Board.

Our interest in the Working Group comes from a simple institutional observation. India does not lack knowledge about its dangerous roads. Black spots are identified, IRC codes and MoRTH protocols exist, and the engineering counter-measures that save lives are well documented and affordable. Identification, however, has outpaced rectification: against 13,795 black spots identified nationally, Parliamentary Standing Committee reporting places permanent rectification at 5,036. In our experience the constraint is rarely intent. It is steady, supervised auditing capacity, and the follow-through that turns an identified risk into completed engineering on the ground.

Crashfree India has chosen to work within this gap between identification and rectification.

Project Rakshak, our audit-to-implementation programme, was designed for precisely this constraint. Trained student teams from IITs, NITs and SPAs, guided and independently reviewed by senior subject-matter experts, audit dangerous locations using instruments anchored to IRC codes and MoRTH checklists. Findings reach the road-owning authority in its own format, and every recommendation is then tracked on a public dashboard until it is built. We do not work around the system, and we do not campaign against it. We add hands, evidence and tools to work the system has already committed to.

The first year of this work has shaped a conviction we believe is relevant to the Working Group's mandate: rectification moves when the authority that owns the road receives implementable, code-anchored proposals, and when progress after approval remains visible to everyone involved. The same logic extends to those most at risk. Roughly seventy per cent of India's road crash fatalities are vulnerable road users, and our audits treat crossings, footpaths, lighting and speeds as designable protections rather than afterthoughts.

A brief picture of the organisation and the work, in numbers:

- Registered as the Vision Zero Trust and co-founded with the Indian Road Safety Council, with a three-year, US$3 million commitment from Cars24. MS Dhoni carries this message to the country as our Goodwill Ambassador.
- 31 high-risk sites audited across 18 cities in year one, by 31 student teams from 20 institutions including nine IITs, with 900+ stakeholders consulted and 17 subject-matter experts guiding and reviewing every audit.
- 25+ written approvals from road-owning authorities; 7 sites fully implemented and 6 more partially built, each tracked publicly. On our audit of Outer Ring Road in South Delhi, PWD built a continuous 1.8 m accessible pedestrian corridor with upgraded drainage, bollards and camera-backed speed-feedback boards.
- SATARK, our AI enforcement platform built with police: over 3,00,000 vehicles screened in Jaipur and Bengaluru, now extended to Gurugram, helping officers focus on the small share of vehicles with the worst records.
- A published evidence line on vulnerable road users, including a 300+ rider study of delivery-rider safety, and four open public data platforms that turn scattered public records into citable evidence.

We see the Working Group as an opportunity to contribute to a national architecture for safe road engineering: one in which audit capacity is dependable, standards are anchored to the codes engineers already use, speed is managed by design, and the journey from identification to rectification is visible end to end. Our audit data, our district relationships in Gurugram, Delhi and Jaipur, and our expert bench are available to the Board as a ready testing ground for the standards the Group will frame.

Our one-page Expression of Interest and a supporting note on the organisation are attached. We would be grateful for the opportunity to contribute, and would be honoured to present this work to the Working Group in whichever form is most useful.

Respectfully,

## Email 2 — Working Group 5

**Subject:** Expression of Interest of Crashfree India — Working Group 5: Post-Crash Care, Policy Standards & State Implementation

Respected Chairperson and Members of Working Group 5,

Crashfree India would like to submit its Expression of Interest for induction into Working Group 5 of the National Road Safety Board.

Our interest in the Working Group comes from a simple institutional concern. India has developed laws, schemes and standards for emergency treatment, crash compensation, victim assistance and post-crash administration. Their value, however, depends on whether different institutions can function as one coherent system for the person affected by a crash.

A victim or bereaved family does not experience post-crash care as a single government programme. They encounter hospitals, police authorities, district administrations, enquiry officers, insurers, legal-services institutions and tribunals. Each may perform an important function, but the overall process can become difficult to navigate when responsibilities, timelines and information are fragmented.

Crashfree India has chosen to work within this gap between legal entitlement and administrative delivery.

Our engagement with crash compensation began through legal and policy research, extensive interaction with victims and families, and consultations with practitioners and public institutions. That work increasingly led us towards implementation. We have engaged with district administrations, police authorities and District Road Safety Committees to understand how claims move through the system, how files are reviewed and why eligible families may remain unable to access statutory relief.

This experience has shaped our larger understanding of post-crash reform. The challenge cannot be addressed only by amending a provision, issuing a circular or creating another digital form. It requires a system in which the responsibilities of each institution are visible, information moves reliably, the claimant understands the next step and public authorities can identify where cases are getting delayed.

Our ongoing engagement with the Rajasthan Lead Agency reflects this approach. Crashfree India is supporting the design of a digital case-management portal for the Hit-and-Run Compensation Scheme. The proposed platform is intended to make the statutory process easier to access and administer through guided filing, clearer document pathways, stage-wise visibility and administrative dashboards. It is being conceptualised as an enabling layer around the existing public system, with human authorities retaining responsibility for every substantive decision.

For us, the portal is part of a broader institutional idea. Technology should help reveal bottlenecks, reduce avoidable errors, improve communication with families and create evidence for administrative reform. It should not reproduce the same fragmented process on a screen.

A brief picture of the organisation and the evidence base, in numbers:

- Registered as the Vision Zero Trust and co-founded with the Indian Road Safety Council, with a three-year, US$3 million commitment from Cars24. MS Dhoni carries this message to the country as our Goodwill Ambassador.
- 'Justice Unserved' (January 2026), our study of the hit-and-run compensation pipeline, launched at IIT Delhi, its findings presented to the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and covered by national press.
- 'Deposited But Not Delivered', our follow-up brief: nearly ₹986 crore of court-awarded compensation lies unclaimed across just five High Court jurisdictions, a bottleneck the Supreme Court took note of in 2025.
- The scale of the gap, from the latest Lok Sabha reply of April 2026: 68,584 hit-and-run crashes were recorded in 2024, while 9,813 compensation claims were registered in FY 2025-26, roughly one claim for every seven crashes, even after a sharp improvement in uptake.
- Working seats on four District Road Safety Committees, in Gurugram, South-West Delhi, North-West Delhi and Jaipur, with MoUs with Jaipur Traffic Police and Gurugram Police, and file-level pipeline support formally tasked to us in signed committee minutes.
- Tools families use today: Aasha, an AI legal companion on WhatsApp; a compensation calculator built on Supreme Court principles; trauma-ward legal helpdesks that supported over 100 victims in their first four days; and plain-language guides and comics in Hindi.
- The Pre-Hospital Care Primer (September 2025), our EMS evidence line benchmarking ERSS-112 response and state innovations, with contributions from Dr. Sushma Sagar of the AIIMS Trauma Centre.

Crashfree India would bring to WG-5 a combination of legal analysis, policy research, product thinking, field engagement and implementation support. Our young multidisciplinary team includes professionals educated at institutions such as the IITs, Tata Institute of Social Sciences, Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and the University of Delhi. The team brings experience across technology, development studies, psychology, law, and public policy.

This diversity matters because post-crash care is not only a legal question. It is also a question of institutional design, user behaviour, access, public communication, data and administrative coordination. A legally sound framework may remain ineffective if it is difficult for a claimant to understand. A well-designed portal may fail if it is disconnected from district workflows. A strong policy recommendation may have little value unless it can be translated into tools that officials can use.

We see the Working Group as an opportunity to contribute to a larger national architecture for post-crash care. Such an architecture should connect emergency response, hospital treatment, documentation, compensation, rehabilitation and grievance resolution. It should offer national coherence while recognising that States and districts may follow different administrative arrangements.

Our one-page Expression of Interest and a supporting note on the organisation are attached. Crashfree India would be grateful for the opportunity to contribute its research, institutional engagements, technology work and victim-facing perspective to this process. We hope to support the NRSB in building a post-crash system that is easier to navigate, clearer in responsibility and more responsive to the dignity and needs of victims and their families.

Respectfully,

Akhtar Hussain
Mission Lead, Crashfree India
akhtar@crashfreeindia.org · crashfreeindia.org
