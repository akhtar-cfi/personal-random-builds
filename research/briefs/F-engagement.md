## FILE 1 — CFI_Guidebook_2025.pdf (fileId 1jxTesGF3nZHnG0qWUyP_d5fb6zNdwGkW)

**What it is:** Despite the "Guidebook" filename, content is a 2-page Hindi-language public-education flyer/poster set titled "Crash Claim" (दुर्घटना के बाद मुआवज़ा: आपका कानूनी अधिकार — "Compensation after a crash: your legal right").
**Audience:** Hindi-speaking crash victims and their families; general public.

**Page 1 — Compensation rights:**
- Compensation is a legal right for serious injury or death in a road crash.
- Applies even if: driver fled; victim was pedestrian/cyclist/passenger; fault unknown; victim doesn't want to accuse anyone; other vehicle uninsured.
- What compensation covers: treatment/hospital costs, financial loss during treatment, temporary/permanent injury, assistive devices (wheelchair, crutches), hit-and-run cases, death — funeral costs, loss of future earnings, financial support to family, emotional/family loss.
- 4-step process: (1) File FIR at nearest police station; (2) Get free legal aid from nearest DLSA or legal aid clinic; (3) Collect all documents needed for the claim; (4) File claim at district MACT (Motor Accident Claims Tribunal).
- Documents: Aadhaar copy, medical bills, bank passbook, disability certificate (if any), FIR copy.
- Help: NALSA Helpline 15100 (National Legal Services Authority); DLSA (District Legal Services Authority); CFI helpdesk helpdesk@crashfreeindia.org; WhatsApp +91 7838059367.

**Page 2 — Cashless (free) Treatment Scheme for Road Accident Victims, 2025:**
- ₹1.5 lakh coverage, up to 7 days, at designated hospitals — free cashless treatment as a right.
- Must reach a designated hospital within 24 hours of the crash.
- Good-Samaritan message: citizens can help victims without fear — no legal obstacle; helper not required to give name/number.
- Dial 112: to ask for the designated hospital, call an ambulance, report the crash.
- Non-designated hospitals must give immediate treatment but scheme coverage not guaranteed there.
- Warning: police report must be filed — if not confirmed within 24 hours, the scheme won't apply and the victim may pay the full bill.
- Escalation: designated hospital refuses → District Grievance Officer (get number from District Collector); other treatment/hospital problems → dial 14555 (NHA helpline).
- Same CFI helpdesk contacts.

## FILE 2 — CFI_ComicFinal.pdf (fileId 1FgspatlWCKWswmkbwaykvAk4ODDOWcYR)

**What it is:** English-language comic book, "Know Your Rights with Aasha — A simple guide to compensation after a road crash." Features a guide character, **Aasha**, described as "Your Legal Ally" — "a guide who helps crash victims understand compensation and free legal help." (Note: "AASHA" also appears in the Dhoni deck as CFI's 24/7 multilingual AI helpdesk — the comic character and the AI helpdesk share the name/brand.)
**Audience:** crash victims / legal heirs; public legal literacy.

**Content:**
- Eligibility: grievous injury; legal heirs of deceased (spouse, children, parents). Compensation possible even if vehicle fled, no insurance, fault not established, pedestrian hit. "Fair compensation isn't optional — it's your entitlement."
- Three compensation pathways:
  1. **Hit & Run Scheme** — applies when driver/vehicle fled and vehicle not identified even after police investigation. Amounts: ₹2,00,000 (death), ₹50,000 (grievous injuries). Process: police must inform victim of the scheme and transfer file to Claims Enquiry Officer/Tehsildar office; victim visits CEO with FIR copy, Post Mortem Report/Injury Report, Death Certificate, Bank Passbook, ID Proof (Form I submission). If vehicle identified later, claim shifts to Sec 164 or no-fault.
  2. **No-Fault, Section 164** — no need to prove fault; file with MACT. Fixed relief: ₹2,50,000 (grievous injury), ₹5,00,000 (death). Immediate relief; can still apply later under Sec 166 for higher compensation once fault proven.
  3. **Fault-Based, Section 166** — full compensation when negligence is clear (drunk driving, overspeeding, wrong-side driving); covers medical costs, income loss, emotional suffering, future care; for long-term losses / loss of main earner.
- MACT claim documents: FIR, death certificate, Aadhaar, disability certificate, birth proof, dependency certificate. Tagline: "Documents ready, relief steady!"
- Legal help: private lawyer or free assistance via DLSA. NALSA helpline 15100. CFI helpdesk +91 7838059367 / helpdesk@crashfreeindia.org.
- Closing: "COMPENSATION IS YOUR LEGAL RIGHT."

**Audience:** students/young professionals (IITs, IIMs, NLUs, top public policy institutes) + policy experts; newsletter itself is for CFI's community/stakeholders.

**Funnel numbers (ACTUAL):**
- 700+ registrations → 250+ concept notes from across India.
- 37 teams worked with expert mentors over 4 weeks to develop detailed policy proposals.
- 24 experts engaged for mentorship & evaluation (from Govt. of Rajasthan, NIUA, OMI, NASSCOM, CSEP etc.).
- 10 finalist teams (selected through three rigorous rounds) presented at the finale across 6 tracks.

**Six tracks:** (1) Speed Management in Urban Areas; (2) Delivery Rider Safety (most-discussed — 3 finalists); (3) Road Safety Data Transparency; (4) Crash Claim Compensation Reform; (5) Local Blackspot Identification; (6) On-Road Emergency Protocols.

**Track highlights / solutions discussed:**
- Delivery rider safety: algorithmic pressure, long hours, fatigue. Team Strategists (IIM Bangalore) — fatigue management via rider pods (Chennai model); Team A² (Kolkata) — "SafeMile Protocol" aligning ETAs to safe-speed benchmarks & tiered delivery windows; tiered permit + mandatory training; digital enforcement of rest breaks; rider-side compliance dashboard. Context stat: India's gig/platform workforce projected ~23.5 million by 2029–30.
- Data transparency: privacy-preserving pipeline to release de-identified e-DAR with documented schema; "Sadak Suraksha" — AI-driven road-safety hotspot map layering crashes with geometry, land use, school/hospital zones, vulnerable-density. Praised by Abhay Damle.
- Blackspot identification: citizen-led geo-tag reporting apps, police/ULB verification workflows + rapid fixes, open interface to feed state blackspot lists & budgeting. Dr. Tiwari flagged inconsistent blackspot definition as a core gap.
- Emergency protocols: AI-enabled crash detection with severity flags integrated into ERSS-112; dispatch automation & triage prompts; bystander participation toolkit; detection-to-arrival monitoring data loop.
- Compensation reform: First Runners-Up team **Safer Miles Initiative** — blockchain-enabled platform issuing smart contracts for instant compensation, linking accident feeds with MACT databases (lauded by Dr. Geetam Tiwari and R. Venkat Ratnam); another team — tripartite reform linking FIRs, medical records, UPI, e-DAR into unified digital workflow.
- Speed management: data-led automated enforcement on priority corridors; context-specific limits (time-of-day, land use); public compliance dashboards.

**Pre-Hospital Care Primer (launched at the finale):** "Pre-Hospital Care in India: A Primer on ERSS-112 Implementation, Global Comparisons and Regional Innovations." Key insights: ERSS-112 operational across all 36 states/UTs with GPS-enabled dispatch, yet on core highways ambulance arrival time is 25–35 minutes; global systems achieve call-to-hospital within 60 min vs Indian highways total episode time 55–75 min; Golden Hour benchmarks need India-adapted timeframes; promising state models MEMS (Maharashtra), TAEI (Tamil Nadu). Quote from Dr. Richa Ahuja (Asst. Professor, IIT Kharagpur, expert contributor). Primer is a segue into a planned detailed national primary assessment of pre-hospital care and ERSS-112 (TARGET/planned; open invitation to collaborators).
