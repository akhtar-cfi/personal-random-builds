## (a) The problem CFI documented

**Core paradox:** India has comprehensive road crash compensation law, but the system is fragmented and largely inaccessible. "The law exists, but implementation is failing victims." ~1.7 lakh road crash fatalities in 2023 (MoRTH), yet in FY 2022-23 the General Insurance Council received only 205 hit-and-run claims against ~25,000 eligible hit-and-run crashes/year. Supreme Court itself noted "a negligible number of victims benefitting from schemes" due to "low levels of awareness among the public and weak dissemination by authorities" (S. Rajaseekaran v. UoI, Jan 2025).

**Three legal routes exist** (all mandate direct bank transfer, free DLSA legal aid, SC-monitored timelines):
1. Hit-and-Run Compensation Scheme 2022 (in force Apr 1, 2023; S.161/163 MV Act): no-fault, ₹2 lakh death / ₹50,000 grievous injury; administered by Claims Enquiry Officers (CEO, ≥Tehsildar/SDO rank), Claims Settlement Commissioner sanctions within 15 days, GIC disburses within 15 days.
2. No-Fault Liability, S.164 MV Act: fixed ₹5 lakh death / ₹2.5 lakh grievous injury via MACT.
3. Fault-Based, S.166 MV Act: unlimited, negligence must be proven; Sarla Verma multiplier method; non-pecuniary damages possible.

**Five critical gaps framework (Justice Unserved):**
1. **Awareness** — 70% of low-income-household (LIH) and 63% of high-income (HIH) respondents unaware of compensation clauses (World Bank survey). Police mandated as informers but lack capacity; hospitals don't inform; legal aid invisible outside cities; "ambulance chasers"/unregulated intermediaries fill the vacuum (BCI suspended 28 advocates in 2021 for fake MACT claims).
2. **Timeliness** — average compensation timeline 3.6 years (Azmi & Ram 2023), some cases 10–15 yrs; Justice Karol (SC, 2025): typical MACT case takes 8–10 years, "insurers resist settlement at all stages". FAR due 24h / IAR 30 days / DAR 90 days — any failure cascades. MACT backlog ~10.46 lakh pending cases ≈ ₹80,455 crore pending claims (IRDAI via RTI, May 2024).
3. **Legal efficiency** — 921 of 1,026 pending hit-and-run claims (~90%) stalled on documentary deficiencies (as of July 2024, per Rajaseekaran). Unclaimed MACT funds: ₹282 cr Gujarat, ₹459 cr Bombay, ₹361 cr Goa. Insurance officials claim 50–60% of motor claims settle out of court (mostly National Lok Adalats) — speedy but risk coerced/discounted settlements. NLA Dec 2025 at P&H HC: 303 cases settled, ₹13.36 cr awarded; NALSA 2020: 10.4 lakh cases in one event.
4. **Adequacy** — minimum-wage-default valuation undervalues informal workers; non-pecuniary damages skipped at lower levels. SC corrections: Kajal (₹9L tribunal → ₹23L HC → ₹51.62L SC); Hitesh Nagjibhai Patel (8-yr-old, ₹8.65L HC → ₹35.9L SC, Sep 2025); Deepak Singh (Feb 2025, students' notional income > minimum wage); Parminder Singh v. Honey Goyal (Mar 2025, attendant/diet/physio costs).
5. **Equity** — only 14% of LIH receive insurance compensation vs 24% HIH (World Bank); 24% LIH sold assets post-crash vs 7% HIH; ~half of two-wheelers uninsured (Shenoy: "insurance is more expensive than occasional fines"); gendered burdens on female-headed households; urban/documented claimants favoured.

**"Institutional white space":** minimal civil-society engagement in post-crash legal access; no systematic outreach at hospitals/police stations; compensation treated as downstream legal matter, not part of post-crash response; near-absence of victim-centred empirical research — administrative data shows filings/awards but not where eligible cases drop off.

**Jaipur/Rajasthan ground truth (decks 2 & 3):** claim crosses six institutions FIR→payment, every hand-off manual. Six leaks: (1) intake starts late — cases surface only at 2-monthly police–DLSA meetings (~60-day latency), not at FIR; (2) ~50% of hit-and-run FIRs have no usable phone number — half of eligible families never reached; (3) document inflation — court-agreed standard is 7 documents (per Rajaseekaran), Jaipur SDM office demands 11 (injury) / 13 (death), plus GIC adds more; (4) intermediary capture — "vakeel"-driven process in a large share of cases; advocate-supplied MACT-sealed passbooks trigger objections; families report cuts; (5) reject-on-objection at GIC — no single-pass check, no SLA; a single objection returns file to SDM, resets the clock (16 of 22 pending Jaipur cases stuck on MACT-affidavit objections); (6) manual & slow — SDM cell scans/splits ~5 files/day; GIC portal itself slow. Gurugram audit found the RTO assumed a verification role not mandated by the central scheme.

## (c) What CFI actually DID, and results

1. **Research (Justice Unserved, Oct–Dec 2025 consultations → Jan/Feb 2026 brief).** Three evidence streams: 50+ source literature review; 20+ expert consultations (judges, insurers, lawyers, police, legal aid); field visits to MACTs and legal aid authorities; initiated structured Delhi-NCR victim survey. Produced the five-gap framework and "first comprehensive victim-centred evidence base" positioning. Launched at IIT Delhi. Result: media (IndiaSpend) now cites CFI's compensation analysis directly.
2. **Hit-and-run pipeline audits.** Gurugram: mapped police/Tehsildar/RTO/SDM stages, reviewed 102 files since 2022 — only 2 settled; exposed non-mandated RTO verification role. SW Delhi DRSC: joined first meeting, audited 10–12 live files. NW Delhi DRSC: integration done, workflow being operationalised. Strategic pivot stated in Founder Update: MACT reform institutionally hard; hit-and-run administrative audit is "the highest-leverage reform track."
3. **Government engagement.** Presented diagnostic to MoRTH framed as "implementation failure, not legislative gap" (sets up administrative fixes via Ministry route). Partnership initiated with Jaipur Traffic Police. July 2026 working draft prepared for Rajasthan Transport Department proposing a phased claims portal + process/document standardisation, with 4 decisions and 6 asks.
4. **Jaipur process mapping (June 2026).** Full claim-chain consultations (DLSA Metro-2, SDM/SDO offices, GIC process). Established the 7-step journey, institutional funnel, 6 leaks, GIC objection patterns; confirmed role/form mapping (SDO = CEO; Forms 1&4 victim, Form 2 SDO, Form 3 DM). Identified Jaipur's two success anchors: active DLSA victim outreach + single SDM coordination cell — both people-dependent, hence the design goal of structure over champions.
5. **Direct victim support pilots.** Hospital emergency-ward legal helpdesks at trauma centres (lawyers + paralegal volunteers): 100+ people supported in 4 days. 300+ reached via awareness sessions. Guidebooks (process overview, per-stage document checklists, DLSA/helpline contacts), comic books, video explainers, community content.
6. **Portal design (proposed, Rajasthan).** Adoption-inverting design: "no stakeholder changes their medium" — family stays on WhatsApp/SMS/call/eMitra; police one screen at FIR (or eDAR trigger); DLSA gets call-assist queue; CEO/CSC get pre-filled प्रपत्र-2/प्रपत्र-3 to sign; GIC gets complete digital file for single-pass review. Automates 5 choke points (traceability, document collection, verification pre-check, form generation, visibility/AI helpline) while "every decision stays with the designated officer." Phase 1 = victim traceability only; Phase 2 = documents + verification + AI helpline + eMitra; Phase 3 = GIC handoff + state scale. Architecture: intake / case spine / office desks / payment+oversight layers; 8-state case machine with SLA timers; independent audit of Phase-1 outcomes proposed. Oct 2025 scheme amendment already mandates electronic processing — portal "implements what the Scheme now requires."

## (d) Aasha chatbot & compensation calculator

- **AASHA (Accident Assistance & Support Help Agent):** 24×7 digital support chatbot guiding victims through immediate post-crash steps, available compensation routes, and required documentation. Includes a **built-in compensation calculator** giving indicative entitlement estimates — explicitly designed to counter misinformation and dependence on informal intermediaries.
- **MACT Compensation Calculator** (listed separately in Justice Unserved): provides indicative **minimum entitlement benchmarks** to strengthen claimants' negotiating position against premature/undervalued settlements. **Expert validation underway prior to rollout** (i.e., not yet launched as of the report).
- **"Sahay"** — the WhatsApp bot branding in the Rajasthan portal mockups ("Crashfree India · Sahay", verified, bilingual Hindi): day-1 auto-triggered intake after FIR, AI document capture with quality checks (e.g., reads post-mortem report photo, flags unclear IFSC), live claim status with push at every state change. Relationship between Aasha and Sahay branding not stated — possibly the same assistant re-skinned for the hit-and-run flow (uncertainty).
- Related AI features: AI-assisted calling to families, AI pre-verification at CEO desk (6 checks + flags, "decision stays human", quality/confidence scores shown), AI helpline in Hindi with human transfer.
