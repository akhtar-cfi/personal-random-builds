## Email 1 — Shankar Maruwada (EkStep Foundation)

**Subject options:**
1. From DIKSHA in 2019 to Crashfree India — seeking your guidance
2. A request for guidance, from a long-time admirer of EkStep
3. Crashfree India: thirty minutes of your counsel

---

Dear Mr. Maruwada,

I am Akhtar Hussain, a 2020 IIT Delhi alum. I run Crashfree India, a road safety nonprofit we are building as a technology and policy institution, with MS Dhoni as our Goodwill Ambassador. I am writing to ask for something specific: your guidance, from time to time, as we grow.

I first came across your work in 2019, during my internship with EY, when I was part of CBSE's DIKSHA programme and EkStep had built the technology infrastructure underneath it. I remember being struck by how quietly that layer carried the whole effort. I have followed EkStep ever since, and the admiration has only grown: Sunbird becoming national infrastructure, People+ai taking on what AI should mean at India's scale, and now Bachpan Manao bringing a hundred-plus collaborators to a child's first eight years. I have long believed that technology, built as a public good, is the instrument for scalable change in India. EkStep is the proof I point people to.

At Crashfree India we are trying to bring that belief to road safety, where India loses over 1.7 lakh lives every year. Our work stands on four pillars today:

- **Project Rakshak.** Student-led audits of dangerous roads, fixed with the authorities that own them. We are now building the technology layer on top: proactive feedback loops that show an authority every recommendation, approval and fix, tracked publicly to completion.
- **Crash compensation.** Fewer than 5 in 100 victims' families receive the compensation the law promises them. We are fixing the pipeline district by district, including a case-management portal we are designing with the Rajasthan Lead Agency.
- **SATARK.** An AI enforcement platform built with traffic police, live in three cities, with over 3,00,000 vehicles screened.
- **Gig rider safety.** Published research and a 300-rider field study on the fastest-growing risk group on Indian roads.

We are at the stage where ambition can outrun experience. We are choosing which programmes become institutions, and learning how to build population-scale public technology without losing the ground game. These are questions you have spent a decade answering. Thirty minutes of your time would mean a great deal, and if the work speaks to you, I would be grateful for the licence to write to you when we face a decision where your experience would show us the way.

A short deck on the work is attached.

With respect,

Akhtar Hussain
Mission Lead, Crashfree India
akhtar@crashfreeindia.org · crashfreeindia.org · New Delhi
