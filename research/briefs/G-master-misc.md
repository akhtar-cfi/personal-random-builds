### Vision language (verbatim, from Credits page)
"A national movement committed to making mobility safer. Our vision is a future where no life is lost on our roads—a nation with **zero road fatalities by 2040**."

### Methodology
Secondary research + publicly available data + **7 exploratory hour-long telephonic consultations** with delivery workers/bike-taxi pilots (Nov 2025–Jan 2026, anonymized). Explicitly notes limitation of n=7.

### Core argument structure
1. Two Wheels, Many Lives → 2. The Gig Rider Equation → 3. Three challenges: (i) Structural & Occupational Risk (time pressure, algorithmic management, economic incentives, city-as-workplace, long hours/fatigue, documentation gaps, e-bike regulatory lacuna, licensing gaps); (ii) Operational & Behavioural Risk (license exploitation, helmetless e-bikers, speed modification, young-male demographics); (iii) Data Scarcity & Silos (platforms/police/PCR/trauma centres each hold fragments) → 4. Two foundational interventions: **inadequate training** and **helmet non-compliance** → 5. Call to Action (shared responsibility across riders, platforms, police).
Thesis phrase: risk is "**systematically produced**" — "neither accidental nor isolated" — through work design × on-road behaviour, multiplied by data silos.

### Key facts & numbers (all [ACTUAL], mostly secondary-sourced)
- ~1.77 lakh estimated road crash fatalities in India in 2024; elsewhere "over 1.72 lakh lives lost each year"; economic losses ~3% of GDP; fatalities up 13.2× in four decades.
- Two-wheelers: >70% of registered vehicles; ~45–50% of crash fatalities; sharpest VRU death rise 2014–2023.
- Gig economy: >23 million workers projected by 2030 (NITI Aayog).
- Global: ~1 in 4 delivery riders involved in a crash (stated-preference lit.).
- Borzo survey (10,900 riders, 20+ cities): 57.3% stressed by 15-min deliveries; 80.6% said 60-min timelines promote safer driving; only 19.4% called 15-min conducive to safety; 56.5% viewed 60-min deliveries as more profitable.
- PAIGAM/UPenn survey (10,000 cab+delivery workers, 8 cities): >80% work 10+ hrs/day; >31% work 14+ hrs. RIGHTS survey (5,000+, 32 cities): >85% work 8+ hrs/day; avg age 28; 94% of another 5,000+ sample aged 21–40. 85% of 5,000+ found 10-min delivery unacceptable; 34% spend 2 hrs/day waiting (Delhi: 50%).
- Earnings: metros ₹25,000–30,000/month full-time; <₹15,000 reported in tier-2/3 (Nagpur); EV rentals ₹200–250/day; incentives ₹500+ for 20+ orders; riders travel 100+ km/day.
- Enforcement: Mumbai ~16,000 cases vs delivery partners (2019); Bengaluru 17,218 cases in one week (Nov 2024; 2.67K on Nov 9 alone); Bengaluru challans vs delivery riders 30,968 (2023) → 52,153 (2024) → 63,718 (first 9 months of 2025); Hyderabad-area: 91 gig-worker crashes reported to Sept 2025 (~1 every 3 days); Kochi ~5 accidents/week involving delivery personnel (2021); Mumbai e-bikers: 211 FIRs (BNS) + 3,155 local-law cases in 4 months (Dec 2024–Mar 2025).
- Helmets: correct use only ~38.4% in Bengaluru (100K-vehicle observational study); correct use of a good-quality helmet reduces severe injury risk by 70% and death by ~40% (WHO); 10.5M+ helmet-related offenses across India Dec 2024–Jun 2025 (Acko), >1/3 of all challans; Bengaluru #1 in repeat offenses, Delhi #1 incidence; 39% of 2024 crash fatalities tied to helmet & seatbelt non-compliance (ToI/MoRTH).
- Licensing/EVs: ~30% of licenses fake (Gadkari, 2019); low-speed EVs (<250W / <25 km/h) need no license, registration, or (practically) helmet; est. 25–30% of last-mile fleets electric in 2025 (GRSF); e-bikes modified from 25 to 40+ km/h.
- Regulation: Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines 2025 mandate 40 hrs training (incl. 6 hrs first aid), up from 30 hrs in 2020 — but apply only to ride-hailing, non-binding, subject to state adoption. MVAG 2025 also puts challan accountability on aggregators (Vahan portal documentation).
- Mumbai crash study (431 food delivery riders): 54.06% had been in a crash; 75.64% in 1–2 near-crashes.
- Chennai: 67% of delivery partners surveyed reported musculoskeletal disorders.
- CFI field test: while testing rider onboarding on one platform, CFI's team was encouraged to complete registration despite declaring the vehicle/plate belonged to someone else — original capability proof of investigative method.
